## 2. Common matrix definitions

**Impact:** expected scope, significance and causal mechanism of the original proposal's effects if its preconditions hold. It is not implementation convenience, achieved ROI or a predicted course mark. **Effort:** combined enterprise dependencies and complexity: data, integration, people/roles, external coordination, process change and governance. Neither budget nor elapsed time alone determines effort.

| Category | Expected business impact | Enterprise implementation effort |
|---|---|---|
| Low | Narrow/local benefit or mainly informational convenience with a limited direct outcome mechanism. | Contained process; few data/role dependencies; limited integration and governance change. |
| Medium | Material benefit in a bounded customer or internal workflow, dependent on adoption and adequate data. | Several data sources/roles; bounded integration and process change with manageable ownership. |
| High | Potentially significant repeated customer, service or organisational effects across a substantial eligible population; depends on a credible causal mechanism. | Multiple interdependent systems/roles or organisations; substantial validation, rollout, controls and sustained ownership. |

**Medium–High** means the supported interpretation spans those categories, not a hidden numeric midpoint. Confidence is reported separately. Broad potential does not mean the effect is validated. Low confidence is not proof of low value. We do not force four candidates into different quadrants.
