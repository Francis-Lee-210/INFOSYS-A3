## 1. Decision, bounded need and evidence boundary

The selected direction is **Billing Review Lite**, a narrowed adaptation of Rae's Spark NZ Financial proposal. Four actual asynchronous #11 comments now confirm that direction; the [dated canonical update](../../decisions/A3_Candidate_Comparison.md#11-dated-confirmation-update--16-october-2026-nzdt) records participants, timestamps, direct links and conditions. These are confirmations of the PR #22 scope, not a meeting, approval of every analytical judgement, Staff endorsement, or final #15 ratification. Rae explicitly warns that her projected A2 benefits must not be presented as prototype results. Knowledge Handover remains the preferred fallback, not a second implemented solution.

**Original need:** in Rae's publicly reconstructed annual mobile-review journey, a customer must find and interpret usage/spend before judging plan fit; Spark's Financial interest is a *possible* reduction in servicing cost/mismatch and retained contribution, subject to validation [R1 §§3.1–3.7; R2 §§1.1,6.3,7.4; R3 §§2–4]. Those assignments do not establish Spark's current internal process or a causal financial loss.

**Lite need statement (formulated now):** a reviewer needs to understand the reported amount, currency, processing date, source and limits of **one billing-request record**, then record what they intend to do next, without mistaking uncertain evidence for payment, complete history or a completed account action.

The retained mechanism is **present qualified evidence → make its sufficiency/limits understandable → record an explicit intention**. A useful micro-outcome is traceable comprehension and a correctable intention, not a suitable annual plan decision. Spark/Financial is credible context because the original proposal addresses evidence interpretation at a financial review point; it is **not** proof of unchanged business purpose merely because the company/domain names match. The training API does not link this record to a Spark customer or annual review invitation [D1–D3]. The user/operator in the prototype is a reviewer, not authenticated Spark account ownership.

## 3. Route and model decision: not automatic justification

**Option 2 remains the working classification**, supported by the retained mechanism and an explicit modification map, not by the Spark label alone. The reduction from an annual plan-fit intervention to a single request-review micro-interaction is substantial. It does **not** independently test Rae's refined annual H2. This design can test whether reviewers understand one financial record and its limits; the team must decide in #15 whether that is sufficient business continuity for the final story, using #10's live build and #9 findings. If not, revise #4/#12/#15 and the canonical decision before freeze; do not silently turn it into a procurement tool or hide the mismatch.

Course rule basis [C1/C2]: Options 1/2 may reuse a member's TO-BE with improvements disclosed. Under Option 3, selecting another member's existing solution uses **its applicable model**. A **wholly new** solution requires new AS-IS and TO-BE plus change details. Not every Option 3 requires two newly invented models. The local V3 rubric's broad Option 3 wording must be read with the archived specific Staff answer; this task could not retrieve the authenticated current Ed/Canvas text, so #15's owner must check for later amendments. No specific Staff approval of Lite is claimed.

**Material handoff findings:** (a) remove suitability/change/Care/KPI semantics, not just diagram boxes; (b) required versus optional evidence are different gates; (c) a new item read invalidates an unconfirmed intention; (d) a receipt's source is historical, not the latest connection; (e) R2's GenAI-design transcript is dated 17 August although its header refers to interview findings dated 24/26 August—ask Rae to reconcile provenance/chronology rather than backdate a design story; (f) R3 Table 9 shows ≈3.3 months payback while R4 slide 14 rounds to ≈4 months—neither is a measured result and neither belongs in a Lite benefit claim. ## 5. Visual and interaction rationale

- **Hierarchy:** title the task as understanding one billing request, not choosing the “best” plan. Screen 1 identifies the source and bounded loaded examples; screen 2 puts amount/currency/basis before intention. Short prose states what the evidence does *not* establish [R2 Appendix B Q8–Q12; D1].
- **Financial readability:** preserve exact signed decimal strings and explicit currency on every amount, never default NZD, round to “$250” or sum unlike figures. A credit example prevents a positive-spend assumption. Processing/booking date is labelled independently from payment/service coverage [D1/D2].
- **Trust and source:** synthetic provenance is persistent. Historical receipt carries the original reviewed source, date and limitations even when a later connection scenario fails. A badge alone is insufficient; the explanation repeats the boundary near the decision [I2/I3].
- **Control:** consistent Back/Continue/Confirm labels; Back corrects selection/evidence/choice. No fourth comparison page, Care portal or operational account controls. Keep and Later remain legitimate intentions, not conversion pressure [R2 §8.2; R3 §3.4 as a changed—not reused—rule].
- **Error and pending:** “No records” is not failure or zero spending. Required-data failures block interpretation. Optional gaps remain qualified; known conflicts constrain intentions. Refresh returns unconfirmed choices to checking evidence and clears the choice; both display and action guards prevent stale confirmation [I3].
- **Visual treatment:** restrained plum/magenta accents suggest continuity with existing Lite without a Spark logo/official-brand claim. Text labels, not colour alone, distinguish warnings. Flexible cards and stacked amount labels on mobile avoid hiding currency/basis. Focus outlines and status targets support explicit navigation/recovery; detailed research identifiers stay outside the user-facing flow.
- **Simulation scope:** [wireframe](issue-12-wireframes.html) has only synthetic examples, manual scenario resolution and a memory-only illustrative receipt. It uses no network, real key or browser persistence. Reopening is not proof of the app's validated storage. Setup controls sit outside the three-screen interaction.
