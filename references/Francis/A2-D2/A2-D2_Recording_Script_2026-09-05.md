# A2-D2 recording script

20 spoken segments, one per slide. All dollar amounts are in New Zealand dollars. Source details are separate from the spoken text.

## 1. PlanFit and the investment decision

Currently, customers can see their mobile usage, but may still wonder: is this the right plan for me? I propose PlanFit, a feature within MySpark that turns usage and spending information into a clear recommendation: keep the current plan or consider a better-fit Spark option.

## 2. Spark's mobile lead narrowed

Spark's group connection share fell from forty-two percent in June 2022 to thirty-eight percent in June 2025, while One NZ reached thirty-seven. That narrower lead makes retention a priority for this proposal. My focus is the customer decision about whether their plan still fits.

## 3. A customer-experience priority

SPK-30 prioritises core connectivity and better customer experiences. FY26 mobile service revenue grew one point one percent, and broad interaction NPS reached plus forty-two. PlanFit translates that strategic direction into a specific customer decision, using customer-intimacy thinking to explain the fit of a mobile plan.

## 4. A focused process scope

The proposal focuses on Spark-brand individual consumers in New Zealand, across pay-monthly and prepaid plans. It covers the journey from a recognised mismatch or review request to a decision. Network remediation, enterprise sales, Skinny, wholesale and MVNO operations sit outside this engagement.

## 5. A clear conclusion at the decision point

Public documentation explains how customers browse and change plans. The opportunity I propose is a timely, transparent fit conclusion at the decision point. Phase one will validate the internal journey, including hand-offs and explanation consistency, so the design addresses the customer's actual experience.

## 6. Decision readiness and retention

We will test two questions. Does an explained recommendation improve decision readiness? Does it reduce all qualifying ninety-day voluntary churn by at least fifteen percent relative to the comparison group? These are separate tests. Avoidable churn remains a pre-specified secondary analysis.

## 7. What the interview contributes

Ryan Li rated the explained output four out of five, compared with three for raw information. He valued clear reasoning and a no-change option. This single-case result supports testing the mechanism with more customers. Those two features carry directly into the proposed design.

## 8. The proposed MySpark experience

The proposed MySpark feature would combine approved usage and spend data with rules for eligible Spark plans. It would explain the recommendation and its trade-offs, including when the current plan remains suitable. Customers could correct information or request human help before making their choice.

## 9. A fair comparison with safe choices

Before showing an output, we assign customers to comparison groups. Timing, data, options and help stay consistent. The conclusion and reasons differ. Customers can keep, change, defer, leave or ask for help. We recheck eligibility before fulfilment and measure everyone assigned, including non-completers.

## 10. How we judge the pilot

We target a 15% relative reduction in 90-day churn. Continuation requires that result, a 95% confidence interval entirely above zero, reliable data, adequate statistical power, intact guardrails and updated economics. Delivery and acceptance diagnose the process. We track fulfilment separately.

## 11. Six stages over 39 weeks

The proposed engagement runs for 39 weeks, from mechanism testing through to handover. Process validation, baselines and economics come before design and build approvals. The pilot enrols customers over three weeks, then follows every customer for 90 days before the final continuation decision.

## 12. Ownership and a usable handover

Our proposed team covers process, data, solution, change and programme coordination. Spark appoints the business and control owners who accept each stage. Handover has a practical test: named Spark owners reproduce the analysis and demonstrate how to pause or roll back the service.

## 13. Preparing people for phased adoption

Frontline teams will practise plan explanations, corrections and escalation before release. Rollout then follows the evidence: M7 can authorise at most 10 percent exposure. Later expansion requires replicated results, intact guardrails, and evidence of sustained benefits and economic value.

## 14. Risk responses built into delivery

Each risk has an owner and a defined response. Data or power failures block the pilot. Suitability breaches suppress the output, interface failures stop fulfilment, and severe privacy incidents stop exposure. The steering group can narrow or redesign the work while maintaining the agreed test.

## 15. Customer and business value

PlanFit is designed to give customers a decision they can understand and staff a consistent basis for support. Spark would gain reusable rules and experiment capability. Financial value comes from retained contribution and cashable contact capacity, with Finance validating the benefit before it is recognised.

## 16. Spending follows separate approvals

Spending follows separate approvals. Phase zero caps exposure at 120 thousand dollars. Phase one adds 280 thousand after M1 approval. The full implementation envelope is 2 million, with annual running costs of 650 thousand.

## 17. The base case and the investment choice

The 3-year base case has a negative net present value of 1.114 million dollars. It assumes one-year benefit persistence and fully cashable contact capacity. I therefore recommend a limited diagnostic before larger commitments, with Finance reassessing the economics before design.

## 18. What changes the financial outcome

The conditional upside case reaches positive NPV of 1.902 million dollars. Value depends on eligible volume, churn effect, contribution, persistence and cashability. Phase one calibrates baselines. The pilot tests the effect, with longer follow-up testing persistence.

## 19. The first two approval decisions

Target 5 October for kickoff. M1 checks the mechanism, governance and data access before authorising Phase one. At M2, design requires validated process, baselines and power, plus Finance-accepted updated economics: NPV at least zero and BCR at least one.

## 20. What Phase 0 delivers

Approve Phase zero at up to 120 thousand dollars. You receive a customer-mechanism verdict, an agreed charter, and approved measures and controls. At M1, that evidence gives Spark a clear basis to authorise Phase one separately, redesign the approach or stop.
